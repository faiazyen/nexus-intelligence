# 30-Day LinkedIn Content Calendar

Four pillars: **Dark Funnel education** (DF), **Signal Teardowns** (ST),
**Build in Public** (BP), **Customer Math** (CM). Days 1 to 7 include full post
copy; days 8 to 30 are hooks ready to expand. Voice: direct, short sentences,
no em dashes, no hashtag walls (2 max).

---

## Day 1 (DF): full post

**Hook:** By the time you see an RFP, you have already lost.

Here is the timeline nobody shows you.

T minus 8 months: a CMO names the problem in a meeting you were not in.
T minus 6 months: finance approves a budget.
T minus 4 months: an advisor is already in the room, shaping the spec.
T zero: the RFP goes out. You respond. You are column fodder.

The winner was decided before the document existed.

The fix is not writing better proposals. It is being in the room when the
problem is named. That moment leaves traces in public data: job posts, filings,
leadership changes. You just have to be watching.

## Day 2 (ST): full post

**Hook:** A company just posted a "VP of Digital Transformation" role. Here is what that actually means.

A new executive-level role is never just a hire.

It means the board approved a budget. It means a capability gap got named out
loud. It means every vendor relationship attached to the old structure is now
up for review.

The window opens the day the post goes live and closes about 45 days later,
when the new hire starts building their own shortlist.

If your service touches that gap, the cheapest meeting you will ever book is
the one you request this week. Not after the RFP. Now.

## Day 3 (BP): full post

**Hook:** We are building an intelligence platform, so we made it find our own customers. Day 1 results.

We pointed NEXUS at our own ICP: consultants and agencies doing $250K to $2M a
year.

It surfaced 14 accounts with live buying signals. We sent 14 emails, each
referencing the specific trigger. No template blast.

Five replies in 48 hours. That is a 36% reply rate against an industry average
of 1 to 5%.

The product is the pitch. More numbers next week, including the misses.

## Day 4 (DF): full post

**Hook:** 91% of B2B teams buy intent data. 24% see ROI. Where do the other 67 points go?

They go into the gap between knowing and doing.

Your intent tool lights up an account. A rep spends 40 minutes researching it.
Writes a generic email anyway. The moment passes. Multiply by every alert, every
rep, every week.

Data without an action layer is just expensive anxiety.

The teams winning right now automate the whole loop: signal, reasoning, score,
message. The tools that only do step one are selling you a dashboard of missed
opportunities.

## Day 5 (ST): full post

**Hook:** A new COO started at a logistics company on Monday. Every contract her predecessor signed is now under review.

This is the most reliable buying signal in B2B and almost nobody works it.

New executives audit everything in their first 90 days. Vendors, agencies,
consultants, tools. Loyalty to the old guard is zero. Openness to a sharp
outside perspective is at its lifetime peak.

The move: reach out in week two or three. Reference the transition, not your
service. Offer a specific observation about their market. Ask for 15 minutes.

After day 90, the new shortlist is set, and you are back to responding to RFPs.

## Day 6 (CM): full post

**Hook:** One consultant. One signal type. $180K engagement. Here is the math.

A solo consultant we work with tracks exactly one signal: incoming CIOs at
healthtech companies between 500 and 1,500 employees.

Three weeks after one appointment went public, she sent a two-sentence note
about the cost of a stalled patient-engagement roadmap. It became a $180K
engagement. No RFP ever existed.

Her total tooling cost for the quarter was less than one billable day.

You do not need more leads. You need to see the right ten accounts before
anyone else does.

## Day 7 (BP): full post

**Hook:** Week one of selling with our own product: what worked, what flopped.

What worked: leadership-change signals. 40% of our replies came from them.

What flopped: news-mention signals. Too weak on their own. We downgraded their
weight in the scoring model this morning. The system now learns this
automatically from outcome data.

Building in public means showing the scoreboard either way. Reply rate for the
week: 24% across 31 sends.
